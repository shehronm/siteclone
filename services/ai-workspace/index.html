When it makes sense

Close the gaps
in everyday work.

01 /

The answer exists, but it is hard to find

Instructions, agreements and service information live across different documents. People open several sources and still need to check which version is current. We start by selecting the information your team can rely on.

02 /

Repeated questions take up the working day

The team keeps explaining the same process or drafting similar replies. We identify those requests and build a workflow that finds the relevant information and prepares a draft for someone to review before sending.

03 /

Context gets lost between a conversation and a task

A meeting or message thread leaves a long history to work through. We design a summary that separates decisions, open questions and next steps so someone can check it and use it in the next stage of work.

04 /

A general AI chat does not know your rules

An answer may sound convincing without reflecting your services, constraints or procedures. We define instructions, approved sources and what to do when information is missing: ask for clarification or hand the question to a person.

05 /

Automation needs clear permissions and ownership

Some documents are restricted, and actions in business tools need oversight. Before connecting tools, we define roles, access boundaries and the operations that require a person's approval.

Practical use cases

Your challenge.
Your team.

01

An assistant for internal knowledge

A team member asks about a process, service or document. The assistant searches approved material and identifies the source when the chosen connection supports it. If information is insufficient, the workflow asks for clarification or routes the question to its owner.

↗
02

Enquiry classification and reply drafts

The assistant identifies the subject of an enquiry, extracts relevant details and prepares a reply using company information. A team member checks the draft, resolves anything uncertain and decides what to send to the customer.

↗
03

Document and conversation summaries

We turn supplied text into a summary of key points, decisions, open questions and possible next actions. The team agrees the format; ambiguous conclusions remain subject to human review.

↗
04

Preparing actions in business tools

An assistant can prepare the information for a task, record or another agreed action. We connect through the interfaces available in the target system. Where approval is required, a person first sees exactly what is about to change.

build together.

A defined workflow and quality criteria

We document who the assistant supports, which requests it handles and how its output is accepted. We also identify situations that must return to a person.

⊞

Connected knowledge sources

We configure the agreed documents and available connections. We identify content owners, an update process and how outdated material should be handled.

↔

Instructions and response formats

We define the assistant's purpose, output structure, tone and handling of uncertainty. Different tasks receive separate instructions where one workflow is not sufficient.

↳

Roles and access rules

We agree who can use the workspace, which sources they may access and which actions are permitted. We check how those boundaries are supported by the chosen platform and connected systems.

⊕

Tool connections and approval steps

We connect the workflow to agreed tools where the required APIs and permissions are available. We define approvals, error notifications and how exceptions are handled manually.

Checks and team handover

We check the workflow against an agreed set of routine and difficult requests. The handover explains how to use the assistant, update its sources and review answers that do not meet the criteria.

Questions & answers

Before
we begin.

01

What does AI Workspace mean in your approach?

+

It is a workspace where assistants carry out defined tasks using approved knowledge and tools. Our approach includes workflows based on Dust. We choose a suitable configuration after reviewing the process, information sources and permissions.

02

Do all our documents need to be organised first?

+

The first workflow needs a limited set of current, dependable material. We identify that set, its owners and its update rules together. A large, unprepared knowledge base should not be connected without checking its content and permissions.

03

Who will be able to see internal information?

+

We agree user and assistant permissions before connecting data. We check the boundaries supported by the platform and each source. Data handling requirements are discussed separately: a feature being available does not mean that every type of information is suitable for that service.

04

Can the assistant make mistakes?

+

Yes. We therefore define approved sources, check answers against an agreed sample and plan for missing information. Important replies and actions require human review where agreed. Neither a knowledge base nor instructions can guarantee that errors will never occur.

05

Can AI connect to our CRM and other tools?

+

This depends on the APIs, connections and permissions available in each system. We first define what the assistant needs to read and which actions it should prepare. We then agree approvals and error handling, assessing each connection individually.
